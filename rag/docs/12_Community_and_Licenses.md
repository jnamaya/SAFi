# SAF Explained: Community and Future Vision

## Open source model
The project is committed to transparency and collaboration through a dual license structure.

- The SAF framework (theory) is released under an MIT-style license with an attribution requirement. Anyone may study, adapt, teach or build on the ideas; the one condition is credit: "Self-Alignment Framework (SAF), originally structured by Nelson Amaya", including where terminology has changed.
- SAFi (the software) is released under the **GNU Affero General Public License v3 (AGPL-3.0)**, not plain GPLv3. The authoritative text is the LICENSE file in the SAFi repository.

## Why Affero rather than ordinary GPL
Standard GPL copyleft is triggered by distribution. Someone could take SAFi, modify how the Will gates decisions or how the Conscience scores values, run the result as a hosted service, and distribute nothing — owing no source to anyone, while every user of that service is governed by a black box.

AGPL's network provision closes that gap: run a modified SAFi as a service other people interact with, and those people are entitled to the modified source. Governance logic cannot be forked into secret and then pointed at the public. The licence choice is therefore not incidental to SAF's argument about transparency; it is that argument given legal effect.

Open here means inspectable and answerable. It does not mean unowned or unconditional — the attribution requirement is real, and the copyleft is inherited by anything built on SAFi.

## Long term vision
The vision is a world where complex systems, including AI, corporations, and public institutions, use a common auditable protocol for self regulation. This fosters coherence, transparency, and accountability across all sectors.

## Getting involved
The best way to start is by exploring the documentation and resources provided in the SAFi project. Community participation, research, and discussion are encouraged.
