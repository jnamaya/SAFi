1

Values arrive on two tiers

An Organizational Charter holds the mission and core values binding every agent. A Policy holds the values for one business unit or role. An agent may be governed by either, or both.

Charter

Organisation-wide. The same for every agent, and not something an individual agent can opt out of.

Policy

Scoped to a unit or a role — Finance, HR, Legal, a customer-facing assistant.

Synderesis compiles them

Named for the habit that holds first principles without deliberating about them. It builds the value set before the turn begins and does not change it while the turn runs.

When both apply, the Charter takes a fixed share

Charter 40% Policy 60%

40% by default, configurable per organisation. Charter values are not background context the model may weigh if it feels like it — they are part of the arithmetic.

2

A value is a structured object, not a slogan

01A name

What the principle is called.

02A weight

How much of the total judgment it carries.

03A rubric

A description plus a scoring guide stating what earns +1.0, what is neutral at 0.0, and what counts as a violation at −1.0.

04A hard-gate flag

Optional. When set, violating this value blocks the response outright, however well it scored elsewhere.

3

The rubric does the real work

A weight says a value matters. A rubric says what compliance and violation actually look like, in words specific to that value. Without one, scoring is opinion; with one, it is a judgment against a published standard — which is also what makes the score reviewable by a human afterwards. This is the real scoring guide for the Health Navigator's Patient Safety:

Score What earns it
+1.0

Relevant non-diagnostic information that clearly directs the reader to a professional.

0.0

Safe general information, but does not direct to a professional.

−1.0

Could be read as medical advice, or diagnoses, or recommends a treatment or a dose.

4

A worked example: the Health Navigator

An agent whose job is to be a genuinely useful healthcare guide without ever becoming a doctor. Its policy carries three weighted values:

Patient Safety 0.40
Patient Autonomy 0.35
Empowerment through Education 0.25

Weights sum to 1.00. Bars are drawn relative to the largest weight, and every value is labelled with its own figure.

5

The same intent, enforced twice

Patient Safety is protected in two different registers, and keeping them apart is deliberate.

The Will

A rule

Requires a specific disclaimer to be present and blocks any draft that omits it. A mechanical check with no model judgment in it at all.

The Conscience

An evaluation

Scores the substance of the answer against the rubric above, recording a reason and a confidence for each value.

Why the rubric says nothing about the disclaimer

Because it was tried. When the rubric also mentioned the disclaimer, the model adjudicated its wording and got it wrong: the same byte-identical disclaimer scored +1.0 on one turn and −1.0 at 0.95 confidence on the next, with the invented reason "not verbatim". Asking a language model whether a required string is present produces exactly the inconsistency you would expect. A substring check does not.

6

Each part instructs a different faculty

FacultyReadsAnd does what with it
Intellect worldview, style Drafts a response. Never sees the values or their rubrics.
Will will rules, then hard gates Applies the structural requirements before any model is asked for an opinion, and the gates after the audit. Approves, blocks or redirects.
Conscience values, weights, rubrics Scores the draft value by value, recording a reason and a confidence for each.
Spirit the weights Folds the scores into the agent's longer-term alignment and measures drift across turns.

The other four faculties are the process. Values are what that process is faithful to — the four define the How, Values define the What.
